# Deck Anthropic paper focus revision plan

## Goal

Revise the AI coding skill-formation slide deck so the audience sees it as a direct discussion of Anthropic Research's article "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills," rather than only as a general argument about AI and learning.

## Sources

- Anthropic Research, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills," 2026: `https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills`.
- Shen and Tamkin, "How AI Impacts Skill Formation," arXiv:2601.20245, 2026.
- Existing deck and notes under `presentation/2026-05-27/ai_coding_skill_formation_discussion/`.

## Intended changes

1. Make the cover name the Anthropic article directly.
   - Keep the deck title `The Illusion of Competence`.
   - Add a source-facing subtitle: `A discussion of Anthropic Research, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills"`.
   - Keep the artifact-path vs learning-path motif.

2. Insert an article-question slide after the cover.
   - Title: `The paper's question is not "does AI help?"`
   - Show the article's central distinction: productivity on familiar work versus skill formation while learning a new library.
   - Include the direct question: `Does AI provide a shortcut to both efficiency and skill development?`

3. Make the assessment slide mirror the article's evaluation design.
   - Show four question types: debugging, code reading, code writing, conceptual.
   - Visually emphasize that debugging, code reading, and conceptual understanding are the oversight-heavy skills.
   - Remove the over-specific claim that code-writing questions were excluded.

4. Replace the broad persona slide with the article's six interaction modes.
   - Low-scoring: AI delegation (`n=4`), progressive reliance (`n=4`), iterative AI debugging (`n=4`), average quiz scores below 40%.
   - High-scoring: generation-then-comprehension (`n=2`), hybrid code-explanation (`n=3`), conceptual inquiry (`n=7`), average quiz scores at or above 65%.
   - Keep the caveat that these clusters are descriptive, not causal proof.

5. Revise the strategic-pivot slide into the article's conclusion.
   - Title: `The authors' design implication`.
   - Visible ideas: deploy AI intentionally; protect learning in junior/onboarding work; support learning modes; productivity gains matter, but so does the expertise those gains depend on.

6. Retain the two personal-trace slides, but make their bridge to the article explicit.
   - Tonic trace maps to `iterative AI debugging / artifact path` risk.
   - Coroutine trace maps to `conceptual inquiry / directed debugging` as learning-oriented delegation.
