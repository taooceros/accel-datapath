## Slide content

### Slide: `05 · DISCUSSION` / `What should education protect?`

Purpose: move from tool-design interventions to undergraduate and human-learning policy choices.

Takeaway: if AI changes the cost of producing answers, education must decide which forms of human struggle still need to be protected.

Visible components:

- Top line: `If answers become cheap, which practice loops must remain expensive?`
- Four discussion prompts:
  1. `Assessment`: `What should an intro CS course grade when AI can produce working code?`
  2. `Practice`: `Which tasks should students still do without AI to build debugging judgment?`
  3. `Equity`: `Does AI close gaps, or hide unequal access to mentoring and feedback?`
  4. `Norms`: `When is AI use collaboration, and when is it cognitive offloading?`
- Closing prompt band: `Design one course policy that preserves learning without pretending AI does not exist.`

Visual guidance: use four compact cards in a 2x2 grid. Keep wording short enough to invite conversation, not force reading.
