# Tokio tutorial wording cleanup plan

## Goal

Revise the existing Tokio tutorial slide deck so each visible slide speaks directly to the audience. The deck should show the async/Tokio mechanism through concrete claims, diagrams, code, and short explanatory labels, not through meta text about what the deck is or what it excludes.

## Sources

- Existing deck: `presentation/2026-05-02/tokio_general_tutorial.typ`
- Prior plan: `docs/plan/2026-05-02/06.tokio-general-tutorial-deck.plan.md`
- User direction on 2026-05-04: remove wording such as "self-contained tutorial deck", "No hardware details", and "No stdexec" from slide-visible content because those phrases were planning principles, not audience-facing slide text.

## Scope

In scope:

- Wording-only cleanup of visible slide text.
- Title slide retitled to "Introduction to async and Tokio".
- Remove `Takeaway:` prefixes from slide callouts.
- Replace meta commentary with mechanism-facing statements.
- Improve the `tokio::spawn` contract wording for precision.

Out of scope:

- Changing the deck structure or slide count.
- Adding new technical claims or benchmark/performance material.
- Redesigning diagrams beyond small text-label changes.

## Intended changes

- Slide 1 will introduce async and Tokio directly with a concise mechanism statement: `async fn` creates a `Future`; Tokio polls it, parks it when it returns `Pending`, and wakes it when I/O or timers may progress.
- Slide-visible text should not mention deck construction concerns such as self-containedness, hardware exclusions, `stdexec`, or speaker-note readability.
- Existing callouts beginning with `Takeaway:` will be rewritten as direct claims.
- Notes/callouts that currently explain author intent, such as diagram motivation or layer-reading instructions, will be replaced with audience-facing mechanism explanations.
- The spawn slide will say that `tokio::spawn` stores the future in the runtime and requires the spawned future and output to be `Send + 'static`; values live across suspension points determine whether the future satisfies `Send`.
