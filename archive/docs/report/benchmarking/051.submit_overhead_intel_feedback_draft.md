# Email draft: DSA direct-submit overhead knee

Subject: Feedback request: DSA direct-submit overhead knee below WQ size

Hi [Name],

We are evaluating Intel DSA for fine-grained data-path work where software issues one logical operation per direct MMIO submission. We have a repeatable result that we would appreciate your feedback on.

On a dedicated DSA work queue reported as size 128, a tight single-threaded submit burst starts paying a much higher cost for one additional direct submit when only about 115--117 operations are outstanding. The behavior appears before the nominal WQ size, and the evidence points to a submit/admission effect rather than descriptor contents, payload execution, or completion polling.

Setup:

- Device: `/dev/dsa/wq0.0`
- WQ mode: dedicated
- Reported WQ size: 128
- Submitter: one pinned CPU thread
- Benchmark: `hw-eval --accel dsa --benchmark submit-occupancy`
- Submission mode: one logical operation per direct submission; no DSA `BATCH` descriptor

Experiment shape:

1. Submit `K` prefill descriptors in a burst.
2. Measure the next `wq.submit` call.
3. Drain/observe completions after the measured submit.

The core finding is that the direct-submit cost has a stable knee around `K=115--116` prefill descriptors:

| K prefill | submitted total | noop median submit | 64 B memmove median submit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 112 | 113 | 10 ns | 14 ns |
| 113 | 114 | 10 ns | 12 ns |
| 114 | 115 | 14 ns | 10 ns |
| 115 | 116 | 39 ns | 88 ns |
| 116 | 117 | 102 ns | 101 ns |
| 117 | 118 | 101 ns | 101 ns |

So the first expensive submit appears around 116--117 submitted operations, not at 128.

We tried changing descriptor contents. `noop` and 64 B `memmove` have different descriptor contents and different hardware work, but they show the same knee. We then increased memmove payload size. A 1 MiB memmove changes both descriptor content and completion latency dramatically, but the submit knee still stays in the same place:

| K prefill | 1 MiB memmove median extra-submit | median first-old completion |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 112 | 39 ns | 267.4 us |
| 114 | 68 ns | 159.3 us |
| 115 | 153 ns | 142.1 us |
| 116 | 196 ns | 157.9 us |
| 117 | 199 ns | 146.7 us |

The payload took hundreds of microseconds, but the submit transition still happened around `K=114--116`. This makes us think the threshold follows the number/rate of submitted descriptors, not what the descriptors encode.

We also inserted small pacing gaps between prefill submissions, outside the measured submit call. That moves the knee later:

| pacing gap | first median submit >=100 ns, noop | first median submit >=100 ns, 1 MiB memmove |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 TSC | 114 | 114 |
| 1 TSC | 160 | 160 |
| 10 TSC | 160 | 160 |
| 50 TSC | 192 | 192 |
| 100 TSC | 224 | 256 |
| 150 TSC | none through 320 | none through 320 |

Our current interpretation is:

1. The direct-submit path has an admission/descriptor-consumption threshold below the configured WQ size.
2. A tight userspace producer reaches that threshold around 115--117 outstanding direct submissions on this WQ.
3. The measured submit then waits for admission capacity, producing the ~100--200 ns plateau seen across runs.
4. Payload DMA and completion polling are not the primary cause of this first knee.

What we do not know is whether this threshold comes from hardware queue internals, IDXD driver behavior, reserved entries, portal semantics, descriptor-fetch credits, MMIO/store-buffer behavior, or something in our userspace submit path.

Could you comment on the following?

1. Is a dedicated WQ configured as size 128 expected to backpressure direct submissions around 116--117 outstanding descriptors?
2. Is there an effective direct-submit admission depth that can be smaller than the configured WQ size?
3. Is it expected that changing descriptor contents from `noop` to small/large `memmove` would not move this direct-submit knee?
4. What mechanisms can make a direct userspace submit become more expensive: portal backpressure, software retry/spin, internal credits, descriptor-fetch limits, or reserved slots?
5. Does the pacing sensitivity above match expected DSA behavior?
6. Which counters, tracepoints, driver state, or perf events should we collect to distinguish hardware admission pressure from CPU/MMIO effects?
7. Should DSA `BATCH` descriptors be expected to help here because they reduce portal pressure, descriptor-fetch overhead, or both?

This matters for our project because if this is expected DSA behavior, then a one-operation-per-MMIO design can hit a submit-side bottleneck before reaching the advertised WQ size. That affects how we should compare direct descriptors, DSA `BATCH` descriptors, software batching, and logical concurrency in accelerator-aware data paths.

The main finding we want to validate is: the first bottleneck we observe is direct-submit admission overhead around 116--117 outstanding operations, not descriptor contents, not payload execution, and not completion polling.
