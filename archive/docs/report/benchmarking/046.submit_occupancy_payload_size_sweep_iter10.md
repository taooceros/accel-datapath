# Submit occupancy payload-size sweep, 2026-06-02

## Question

How does Experiment 1's TSC-gap submit-admission knee change across payload sizes: `noop`, 1 MiB, 10 MiB, 100 MiB, and 1 GiB?

## Method

Built release `hw-eval` and ran `submit-occupancy` on `/dev/dsa/wq0.0`, pinned to core 0.

Common command shape:

```text
launch ./target/release/hw-eval \
  --accel dsa \
  --benchmark submit-occupancy \
  --device /dev/dsa/wq0.0 \
  --json \
  --iterations 10 \
  --submit-occupancies <K> \
  --submit-occupancy-gap-tsc <gap> \
  --pin-core 0
```

For memmove payloads:

```text
--dsa-op memmove64 \
--dsa-memmove-bytes <1048576|10485760|104857600|1073741824> \
--submit-occupancy-shared-payload
```

The shared-payload flag reuses one source/destination buffer across descriptors while keeping completion records distinct. This was required for 100 MiB and 1 GiB descriptors; distinct per-descriptor buffers would require impractical memory at K=320.

Sweep shape:

- K: `96,114,128,160,192,224,256,288,320`
- `gap_tsc`: `0,1,10,25,50,100,150,200,300`
- requested iterations: `10`

For 100 MiB and 1 GiB rows, the dedicated WQ often did not produce a drain sentinel completion within the bounded drain wait. The runner now records `drain_sentinel_completed=false` / `drain_sentinel_status=255` and stops that row after the first incomplete drain to avoid reusing buffers that may still be device-visible. Therefore those rows have one collected sample, not ten. `noop`, 1 MiB, and 10 MiB rows have ten samples.

Artifacts:

- Raw JSON: `hw-eval/results/2026-06-02-payload-size-sweep-iter10-perk/`
- Summary CSV: `docs/report/benchmarking/046.submit_occupancy_payload_size_sweep_iter10.summary.csv`

## Drain/completion status

- `noop`, 1 MiB, and 10 MiB: all rows collected 10 samples and all drain sentinels completed.
- 100 MiB and 1 GiB: every row stopped after one sample with an incomplete drain sentinel. This is consistent with dedicated-WQ `movdir64b` submission having no software-visible flow control and very large requests causing silent drops or undrainable bursts.

## Interpretation

The primary result is stable across payload size: the no-gap knee appears at K=114 for all requested workloads. Payload size does not move the first no-gap submit-admission knee earlier, even up to 1 GiB descriptors.

Adding TSC pacing still shifts the knee to larger K values. For the payload workloads, the common pattern is:

- `gap_tsc=0`: K=114
- `gap_tsc=1/10/25`: about K=160
- `gap_tsc=50`: about K=192
- `gap_tsc=100`: about K=256 for payload rows
- `gap_tsc>=150`: no median knee through K=320

This supports the same conclusion as the smaller-payload experiments: the observed knee is a submit-admission / descriptor-consumption pressure point. Larger payloads affect completion/drain reliability, especially at 100 MiB and 1 GiB, but they do not explain the first submit-latency increase.
