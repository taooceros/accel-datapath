capacity: P12, P1, P4, P5 (forks Q2, Q3, Q4, Q7)

Workspace dsa-exp-capacity. Probes:

  • dsa-lab/src/bin/p1_overflow.rs: P12, P1, and capacity for P4/P5. Submits N descriptors (1 MiB memmove), then a marker (Drain or noop sentinel). It polls every empty record in passes, reading the marker first in each pass, and records each record's first-seen pass and time, the final status byte, WQ occupancy (sysfs, an MMIO read of WQCFG OCCUP), and the driver's SWERROR copy after every iteration. A cleanup Drain plus a re-scan ends each iteration.
  • dsa-lab/src/bin/p4_rate.rs: P4 throughput. Windowed noop and 1 MiB memmove (at most W outstanding), plus a noop burst with drop count.
  • x1_knee (reference, unchanged) for the P5 noop knee.
  • Configs: dsa-config/cap-e{1,2,3,4}-wq128.conf and cap-e{1,4}-wq{32,64,96}.conf, generated from 4-engine.conf. They differ only in engine count and WQ size.

Run dirs are relative to dsa-lab-runs/. Unless noted, every run used 8 heaters, uncore_ghz 2.1-2.2 at start and end, core 2.2-3.9 GHz (warm_up), and dedicated wq*.0.

Measurement problems found on the way (they affect other groups)

  1. dsa_lab::touched(len, 0) does not prefault. vec![0; n] is calloc, so the pages stay unmapped. Every 1 MiB memmove to such a destination completes in about 1 us with status 0x03 (page fault, no block-on-fault). Run P12/20261101T021615Z-A-smoke has 96/96 records at 0x03. x1_knee --op memmove has the same problem. Main has been told. My probes fill the destination with 0xa5.
  2. Automatic NUMA balancing (kernel.numa_balancing=1). About 1 s into a process the kernel marks PTEs PROT_NONE. After that, prefaulted buffers fault again in the device (0x03/0x83, bytes_completed=0, fault_addr = page base of src or dst). The faulted descriptors finish at once, free capacity, and inflate the counts: 186-253 "accepted" in P1/20261101T021828Z-B-e4-gap0-r1..r3 and ...-gap200-r1..r3, last iterations only. Fix in both probes: set_mempolicy(MPOL_BIND, {node}) at the top of main. MPOL_BIND has no MPOL_F_MOF, so the task drops out of NUMA balancing. The probes also prefault src and dst again right before every burst. After the fix there were 0 non-success statuses in 253 Drain iterations and 71 sentinel iterations. The runs from before the fix are kept only as status and SWERROR evidence.
  3. The SWERROR sysfs copy is sticky and shared. It survives lab's disable/reload, so it shows the last error from any earlier run by anyone. Examples: 0x531c, operation 1 (another group's batch run); 0x031c with a fault address (my page faults). meta.json swerror_changed is therefore false whenever the previous run left the same value. Use the per-iteration copy that the probe reads, not before/after equality.

P12: Drain descriptor vs noop sentinel (done first)

Question. Does a completed Drain mean every earlier accepted record is already written, and how many completions does the old noop-sentinel method miss?

Predictions (model.js P12):

  • Q4-c: the sentinel misses up to about 3 at N=96, and the capacity rises from ~139 to ~142.
  • "Unbiased": the counts are equal.
  • Spec 3.12: no record may be written after the Drain record.

Runs.

  • N=96 (no overflow), markers alternating, E=4: P12/20261101T021811Z-A-n96-both-r1, ...021812Z-A-n96-both-r2, ...021813Z-A-n96-both-r3 (10 iterations each).
  • Per E: P12/20261101T022123Z-A-n96-both-e1, ...022130Z-A-n96-both-e2, ...022136Z-A-n96-both-e3, ...022142Z-A-n96-both-e4.
  • Old method with its 1 ms bounded pre-wait, N=96/200/320: P12/20261101T022421Z-A-oldmethod-prewait1ms-r1, ...022423Z-...-r2, ...022425Z-...-r3.
  • Overflow (N=200/320) with both markers: the P1 runs below.

Results.

condition Drain: records written after the marker was seen sentinel: missed (after marker) sentinel timeouts
E=4, N=96, 15+15 iterations (before the NUMA fix, but each run < 0.9 s and 0 non-success statuses) 0 in 15 3 in 14 of 15 (always indices 93, 94, 95, 0.2-2.7 us late); 1 unresolved (same pass) 0
E=1 / 2 / 3 / 4, N=96, 5+5 each 0 max 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 (= E-1, always the last E-1 indices) 0
E=4, N=200/320, marker right after burst 0 (first Drain dropped; resubmit at 50 ms completed) marker dropped 24 of 24
E=4, N=200/320, 1 ms pre-wait (old method) 0 0-3, count 137-140 (true 140) 0
all Drain iterations after the fix 253 iterations: 0 after-marker, 0 written during final wait, 0 written after cleanup 0 final failures

Verdict.

  • The Drain is a correct barrier. Across 253 iterations no record became visible after the Drain record. This is a direct count, at pass resolution of about 1 us.
  • The old sentinel undercounts by up to E-1: the last E-1 descriptors are still executing on the other engines when the noop finishes on a free engine.
  • When the WQ is full, the marker itself is dropped. The sentinel then times out in every case. A Drain must be resubmitted (drain() in lib.rs does this; give each attempt longer than the whole backlog).
  • Q4-c is half right. Undercounting exists (≤ E-1), but the true E=4 capacity is 140, not ~142. The old method reproduces the historic "~139" (137-140).
  • Counting method for every experiment below: Drain with resubmission, then a full scan plus a re-scan after a cleanup Drain.

Q7 (completion order).

  • E=1: 0 order inversions by pass in 10 of 10 iterations (...n96-both-e1).
  • E=2/3/4: up to 3/6/13 inversions per iteration.
  • 1 MiB completions arrive in waves of exactly E, every E × ~33 us: first wave at 35 / 68 / 99 / 133 us for E = 1 / 2 / 3 / 4.
  • Q7-a is supported, and refined: completion is in order within one engine and out of order across engines. Evidence: the inversion count plus the sentinel overtaking exactly E-1.

Caveats.

  • Pass resolution is about 1 us. Records first seen in the same pass as the marker are "unresolved" and counted as visible at the marker, which is what the old method did.
  • Q7 for small descriptors (noop) is not tested.

P1: overflow, per-record status, SWERROR (Q2)

Predictions.

  • Q2-b: indices past capacity are never written, no record carries an error, SWERROR shows code 0x53, WQ 0, and Overflow on repeated drops.
  • "No record": sysfs never changes.

Runs (E=4, WQ 128, Drain counting, 3 runs × 10 iterations each):

  • gap 0: P1/20261101T022021Z-A-e4-gap0-bind-r1, ...022024Z-...-r2, ...022028Z-...-r3. The submit loop takes 9-10 us for 200 and 22 us for 320, so the CPU stalls past the knee.
  • gap 200 ns: P1/20261101T022032Z-A-e4-gap200-bind-r1, ...022035Z-...-r2, ...022039Z-...-r3. About 220 ns per submit, no stall.
  • Side B (dsa2), before the NUMA fix: iterations 0-1 of P1/20261101T021828Z-B-e4-gap0-r1.

Results.

  • In all 60 iterations (N=200 and N=320, both gaps): completed = 140. Indices 0..139 have status 0x01 and indices 140..N-1 have status 0x00 (never written). The drop starts exactly at 140 and is contiguous (accepted_after_first_drop = 0). No completion during the burst (pass0_done = 0). 0 error statuses.
  • Peak WQ occupancy read during polling = 128 (= WQ size) in every overflow iteration.
  • SWERROR copy, read by the probe after every overflow iteration: ...,00000003,0000530f.
    • word0 = 0x530f: Valid=1, Overflow=1, Descriptor-valid=1, WQ-index-valid=1, code 0x53, WQ index 0.
    • Operation (bits 39:32) = 0x03, memmove.
    • Once it read 0x530d with operation 0x02 (Valid, no Overflow, Drain): in that interrupt the only drop was the resubmitted Drain. So the copy is refreshed on every new error burst, because the driver acks Valid/Overflow after each interrupt (irq.c:458-463).
  • The lab reads after process exit show 0x530c, Valid/Overflow = 0. The copy was refreshed once more after the ack. [INFERENCE: a second error-cause interrupt read the register after the driver had cleared V/O. Not directly observed.] The lead's F1 run with swerror_after = 0x5308 fits the same picture (a 0x53 drop on WQ 0, V/O already acked).
  • Status bit 7: page-fault records from before the fix read 0x83 or 0x03 (bit 7 = write-fault side). No 0x53 ever appears in a completion record.

Verdict.

  • Q2-b is confirmed by two kinds of evidence: per-record counts (silent drop, exactly the overflow past capacity) and a direct register decode (0x53 / WQ 0 / Overflow).
  • Q2-a (backpressure) stays ruled out: with gap 200 and no CPU stall, the drops are the same.
  • Drops are silent to the submitter: no completion record and no status.
  • The sysfs copy does change (P1 alternative "no record" is ruled out), but it only proves that "a drop happened since the last ack". It cannot count drops.

P4: engines 1..4 (WQ 128) (Q4, Q5)

Predictions.

  • Q4-a: C(E) − 128 = E·m.
  • Q4-b: constant.
  • Q5-a/b: noop throughput does not depend on E.

Runs.

  • Capacity: P4/20261101T0221{16,18,19}Z-A-cap-e1-gap200-r{1,2,3}, P4/20261101T022121Z-A-cap-e1-gap0, P4/20261101T0221{25,26,27}Z-A-cap-e2-gap200-r*, ...022129Z-A-cap-e2-gap0, P4/20261101T0221{31,32,34}Z-A-cap-e3-gap200-r*, ...022135Z-A-cap-e3-gap0, P4/20261101T0221{37,39,40}Z-A-cap-e4-gap200-r*, ...022141Z-A-cap-e4-gap0, P4/20261101T022746Z-B-cap-e1-gap100-r1, ...0227{47,48}Z-A-cap-e1-gap100-r{2,3}, ...0227{50,51,52}Z-A-cap-e2-gap100-r* (N=200, 5 iterations each).
  • Rates: P4/20261101T0222{45,46,47}Z-B-rate-e1-r*, ...0222{48,48,49}Z-B-rate-e2-r*, ...0222{50,51,52}Z-B-rate-e3-r*, ...0222{53,54,55}Z-B-rate-e4-r*, plus side A P4/20261101T022506Z-A-rate-sideA-e1 and ...022507Z-A-rate-sideA-e4.

Capacity C(E). Definition: C = number of descriptors the device accepted while no completion had yet happened (pass0_done = 0, which makes the first-drop index = completed). Gap 100 ns gives 115-120 ns per submit, slower than the ~100 ns write-out cadence, so the CPU does not stall. The whole N=200 burst takes 23-24 us, less than the first 1 MiB completion (≥ 35 us).

2 134 at gap 100 (15/15) 134 (5/5) 134 in 14/15, 135 in 1/15 (pass0 = 0 there too: a completion arrived just after the first drop) 91 = 97 − 6

C(E) − 128 = 3, 6, 9, 12 = 3·E exactly, with or without CPU stall. Two kinds of evidence:

  • the count of written records;
  • the occupancy register, which reads exactly (N + marker) − 3E right after the burst. That is a direct read, so 3E descriptors had left the WQ for the engines.

The +1 values at gap 200 are not capacity. A completion freed a slot during the burst, and one descriptor past the first-drop boundary was accepted (accepted_after_first_drop = 1).

Side A (dsa0, cpu 2), E=1 and E=4: noop 9.41-9.43 Mops/s (W≥16), burst 9.36, W=1 2.73; 1 MiB 30.0-31.5 GB/s. The side difference (98 vs 106 ns per noop) matches the lead's A/B plateau difference.

Verdict.

  • Q4-a supported, with m = 3 per engine. Q4-b (group-shared constant) is ruled out.
  • Q5-a/b: noop throughput does not depend on E (10.2 M/s on B, 9.4 M/s on A, ≈ 1/submit cadence). No noop drops even with one engine, so a single engine runs noops faster than one core can write them. Q5-c (engine) stays ruled out.
  • [new] 1 MiB bandwidth is about 30-31.5 GB/s for every E and window, and even one descriptor at a time (W=1) gets 30 GB/s. The copy bandwidth limit is not per engine. It lies in a shared stage (read buffers, IIO, or memory). Completions come in waves of E every E × 33 us: the E engines each run one 1 MiB descriptor at a time and share the 30 GB/s.
  • [INFERENCE] So the 3 per-engine slots are mostly queued or pipelined descriptors, not 3 concurrent copies. If all 3E were copying concurrently, the first completion would come at about 3E × 33 us, not E × 33 us.

Caveats.

  • m = 3 may be the engine's "Max Work Descriptors in Progress" (spec 4.4, ENGCAP). ENGCAP is not exposed in sysfs here, so this is not verified.
  • 1-engine.conf (the lead's) differs from {2,3,4}-engine.conf in max_transfer_size as well (1 GiB vs 2 MiB). I used my own cap-e* confs.
  • Only 1 MiB was tested. Smaller descriptors may change how many are dispatched per engine.

P5: WQ size 32/64/96/128 (Q3, Q1)

Predictions.

  • Q3-a: C = size + const.
  • Q3-b: C = size.
  • Q1-b: knee ≈ 116 for every size.
  • Q1-c: the knee moves with size.

Runs.

  • Capacity: P5/20261101T0223{20,21,23}Z-B-cap-e4-wq32-r*, ...0223{24,25,26}Z-B-cap-e4-wq64-r*, ...0223{28,29,30}Z-B-cap-e4-wq96-r*, ...0223{32,33,34}Z-B-cap-e1-wq32-r*, ...022335Z-B-cap-e1-wq64-r1, ...0223{39,40}Z-A-cap-e1-wq64-r{2,3}, ...0223{41,42,44}Z-A-cap-e1-wq96-r* (N=200, gap 200, 5 iterations each).

Verdict.

  • Q3-a supported: C = size + 3E (+12 at E=4, +3 at E=1) for every size, and peak occupancy = size. Q3-b is ruled out.
  • Q1-b supported, Q1-c ruled out for the WQ: the knee stays at 116-118 even with a WQ of 32 (smaller than the knee). Evidence: manipulation (size) plus timing (knee).
  • [new] With WQ 32 and noops back to back there are still no drops, because the engines empty the WQ faster than one core fills it.

Suggested corrections to model.js (for the lead)

  • wq:
    • Capacity is exactly size + 3E: 140 at E=4/WQ 128, not "~139".
    • Add: occupancy register (sysfs wq*/occupancy) peaks at exactly size.
    • Add: overflow drops are contiguous from index C onwards, with no error status in any completion record.
    • Status → known.
  • swerr:
    • Badge: "0x53 seen". Status → known.
    • Evidence: sysfs copy 0x530f, op 3; the copy is acked per interrupt and sticky across runs, so it cannot count drops. Page faults (0x03) also land there with a fault address.
  • arbiter:
    • The evidence "WQ slot freed at dispatch" is now direct: occupancy = N + 1 − 3E right after the burst, and C = size + 3E for 4 sizes × 2 E.
    • Status → known.
  • engine_slots:
    • Badge "3 per engine".
    • Status → known for the count. The mechanism (pipeline depth vs queued) stays partial.
    • Replace "139 − 128 − 1–2 ≈ 9–11" with C(E) − size = 3E.
    • Add: each engine copies one 1 MiB at a time (completion waves of E).
  • engines:
    • E is now known per run.
    • 1 MiB bandwidth is about 30-31.5 GB/s for every E and window (even W=1), so copy bandwidth is limited in a shared stage, not per engine.
    • Noop throughput is independent of E.
  • completion:
    • The Drain is validated: 0 late records in 253 iterations.
    • The sentinel undercounts by exactly up to E−1.
    • A sentinel or Drain submitted into a full WQ is itself dropped (timeout); the Drain needs resubmission.
    • "errors=3" in old data is likely page-fault completions [INFERENCE: two 0x03 causes found, unprefaulted calloc dst and NUMA-balancing hint faults].
  • Forks:
    • Q2: b supported → confirmed, with the 0x53 decode.
    • Q3: a confirmed, b ruled out (C = size + 3E at 4 sizes).
    • Q4: a supported (m = 3, linear in E); b ruled out; c partly true (≤ E−1 undercount; true C = 140).
    • Q7: a supported and refined to "in order within an engine (E=1: 0 inversions), out of order across engines".
    • Q1-c: ruled out for the WQ (knee independent of size 32..128).
  • Animation facts:
    • The WQ holds size; each engine takes 3.
    • A 1 MiB copy finishes in about 33 us × E.
    • Noop cadence: about 98 ns (side B) / 106 ns (side A), independent of E.
  • Measurement note for all groups: bind memory (MPOL_BIND or MPOL_PREFERRED) or disable NUMA balancing, and prefault with nonzero writes. Otherwise memmove results become 0x03 faults after about 1 s.

Next questions

  • Does m = 3 depend on descriptor size, for example 4 KiB copies or noops? An occupancy-based probe with a slow first descriptor would show it.
  • Can m be changed with GRPCFG "Work Descriptors in Progress Limit"? Not exposed by this kernel's sysfs (desc_progress_limit is absent).
  • Where is the 30 GB/s cap: read buffers (sweep read_buffers_allowed), the IIO, or memory? Does it rise with two devices in parallel?
  • Q7 for noops: is completion order across engines also scrambled for short descriptors?
