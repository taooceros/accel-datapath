Every completion status other than 0x01 is reported. All probes call set_mempolicy(MPOL_PREFERRED) (lead's NUMA-balancing fix).

Measurement notes (read before using the numbers)

  • Mesh GHz per burst. Reading the CHA counter from the submitter core goes through an IPI that takes 5-130 µs (Q5/20261101T021958Z-A-clocktest2: snap cost and skewed readings such as 1.4/2.9 GHz on a 100 µs window, while node0+node1 still add up to 4.3 GHz). So: short F1-style bursts (~50 µs) get the mesh GHz from busy-spinning 20 ms windows before/after each target (mesh_both), and the fit uses the decay mode, whose bursts are 2-5 ms long (skew < 3-5 %). A sleeping measuring thread also reads below the 0.8 GHz floor (CHA clock gated when the package idles), so mesh_both spins.
  • Core clock is not controlled. The same core runs at 2.2 or 3.9 GHz depending on how many cores are busy. It matters a little (see F1 fit: c ≈ 11-23 core cycles), e.g. cpu2 → dsa0 at 2.2 GHz mesh is 110.9 ns with the core at 2.2 GHz and 106.3-107.5 ns at 3.8 GHz. Always compare rows taken in the same run.
  • Pre-knee submit time is bimodal across runs (~10-18 ns or ~40 ns at K = 32-112) with no link to any knob I varied, including background DMA. I did not chase it; it is not used below.

F1 follow-up: cadence = a + b / f_mesh

Question. Does the steady per-submit cadence follow a + b/f_mesh over the whole 0.8-2.2 GHz range, what are a and b on side A and side B, why is side B faster at the idle mesh, and does the cadence depend on the payload or flags?

Predictions (before running). Mesh-paced transport: one b (mesh cycles) and one a fit every mesh frequency for a given core with rmse at the ns level. If the 64-byte write transport is the limit, then noop, noop without completion record, invalid-flag noops, 64 B and 4 KiB memmove all have the same cadence. For A vs B, the options were: (i) different idle mesh clock per socket, (ii) the device or IIO side differs, (iii) the submitter core's position on the mesh differs.

Getting intermediate frequencies. Heater mixes do not give stable intermediate mesh clocks: with both sockets heated (even 1 spinner each), or with any heater on node0, both meshes go to 2.1-2.2 GHz. With heaters only on node1, node0 stays at 0.80 and node1 sits at ~1.2 GHz (F1/20261101T022654Z-AB-mesh-explore, F1/20261101T022547Z-AB-fit-ab). So the coupling is asymmetric and depends on history. Instead, decay mode heats both sockets (8x1@0+8x1@1), stops the heaters, and then samples 20 000-submit bursts every 40 ms while UFS lowers the mesh to 0.8 (about 450 ms, with ~10-30 samples in between). After ~2.5 s at 0.8 GHz the submit stream itself sometimes pushes UFS back up, which adds more intermediate points.

Runs. Fits use bursts with window > 1.5 ms. No missing completions, no non-success statuses in any noop run.

Side A ~233-243 vs side B ~204-216 ns at the idle mesh: narrowed to the choice of submitter core. The default cores differ in b: cpu2 has b ≈ 169 and cpu34 has b ≈ 145, which is (169 − 145)/0.8 = 30 ns at 0.8 GHz. The core clock (3.9 vs 2.2 GHz, c term ≈ 2-5 ns) explains part of that; I did not pin down the rest [INFERENCE].

P6: two (and more) submitters at once

Question. Is the ~106 ns cadence a per-core serial limit (Q5-a) or a device-entry rate (Q5-b)?

Predictions (model.js, before running). Q5-a: aggregate ≈ 2 × 9.4 M/s, per-thread cadence unchanged. Q5-b: same device capped at ≈ 9.4 M/s, per-thread cadence ≈ 212 ns; a different device is unaffected.

Per-thread cadence is unchanged within 0.5 ns when 2, 4 or 8 cores submit to the same device, and the core-side aggregate scales linearly (19, 38, 77 M writes/s). Evidence 1 (timing plus manipulation): the per-thread cadence while the number of concurrent submitters on the same device changes. Group membership made no difference (1g vs 2g).

Next question. Is the ~41 M/s noop limit per engine (≈10 M/s × 4)? P4 (engine count) can answer it with the 8-core stream.

P7: near vs far submitter (and the per-core sweep)

Question. Does the per-core cadence grow with the core-to-device path?

Predictions (model.js, before running). Q5-a credit round trip: the cadence and the MFENCE round trip grow with distance (far socket adds the UPI latency); the knee does not move. Q5-b: nothing changes. I added one prediction before the k-write test: if each write is a serial handshake, the time for k writes + MFENCE has slope = cadence even with an empty store buffer.

Far adds +65 to +67 ns per write to dsa0 and +54 to +56 ns per write to dsa2 at a 2.2 GHz mesh. The knee stays at 115-117 and nothing is dropped (missing 0, DWQ_FULL 0). The per-write MFENCE slope matches the steady cadence to within 0.6 ns on all four paths, even though the store buffer is empty. Single-write MFENCE grows by +71 / +76 ns. Over the mesh range (decay runs) the far cadence needs both meshes. A fit with one a and separate b for the device-node and cpu-node meshes gives a = 64 / 68 ns (vs ≈ 31 near) and b_dev + b_cpu = 242 / 191 cycles (rmse 1.9 / 1.8 ns; a single-mesh fit has rmse 12-26). The split between the two b's is poorly determined because the two clocks move together (r = 0.82 / 0.95). So the far path adds ~35 ns that are not mesh-clocked (UPI) plus extra mesh cycles [INFERENCE on the attribution].

Core sweep at a 2.2 GHz mesh: node0 → dsa0 spans 97.8-119.4 ns (median 111.7) and node1 → dsa2 spans 89.3-115.8 ns (median 104.6). Among the cores that ran at exactly 2.2 GHz the spread is still 98-119 / 92-116 ns, so it is not the core clock. For all 64 cores the k-write MFENCE slope tracks the cadence (r = 0.992, max |Δ| = 5 ns). The F1 fit shows the spread is in b (mesh cycles): cpu36 127, cpu12 145, cpu2 169, cpu30 188, while a stays at ≈ 31 ns.

Verdict: the Q5-a prediction holds: the far cadence grows by roughly a UPI crossing, the knee stays put, and the per-write fence round trip grows with it. Evidence types: (1) timing plus manipulation (move the core: other socket, other mesh positions), (2) a second independent timing signature (k-write MFENCE slope = cadence for every core and path), and the per-core b from the mesh-clock manipulation. The cadence is therefore a per-core, per-write serial step whose length is the core-to-device path: ≈ 31 ns + b mesh cycles, b set by the core's mesh position, plus ≈ 35 ns + mesh cycles when the path crosses UPI. It is not a full stop-and-wait. A single write takes ~170 ns to be acknowledged while writes complete every ~111 ns, so ~60 ns of each write overlaps with the next [INFERENCE: intercept of the k-write fit]. Caveat: there is one die per socket here (package_00_die_00, package_01_die_01), so "same die / other die" is the core position within the mesh.

Next questions. Which step is serialized: the core's write-combining buffer / uncore request credit, or an ordering ack from the IIO? A test that might separate them: interleave writes to two portals (dsa0 + dsa2) from one core. If the step is per core, the combined rate is unchanged; if it is per destination, the rate doubles.

P11: background DMA vs submission (Q6), and DRAM-streaming CPU heaters

Question. Does DSA's own DMA traffic slow the submit path (cadence, knee, throughput)?

Predictions (model.js, before running). Q6-a (shares IIO/mesh resources with the write-out path): background traffic makes the cadence > 106 ns or the submit intervals longer. Q6-b (unrelated): no change. For the DRAM-heater drops I wrote two hypotheses before running: (d1) DRAM traffic slows the device's descriptor processing, so the WQ fills and writes are dropped as WQ-full; (d2) descriptors are lost some other way (e.g. NUMA-balancing PROT_NONE on completion records), so missing > DWQ_FULL.

The drops are not reproduced: 0 missing in 4 probe runs (≥ 200 bursts of 600 noops under 24 DRAM heaters, with and without the mempolicy fix) and in the lead's own f1_freq_cadence run. The lead's original run (F1/20261101T020451Z-A-heaters) ended with SWERROR code 0x53 (DWQ full, overflow set). Those drops were therefore WQ-full drops, which fits (d1): under same-socket DRAM streaming the device's noop service slows (closed-loop throughput −34 to −40 %, latency +20 to +76 % here), and a 600-noop burst outruns it when the slowdown is deep enough. (d2) has no support: no 0x03 in any run, and the lead's run reports 0x53 (WQ full). Which condition made the lead's run cross the line (side B was not leased, so the other socket may have been busy) is open.

Verdicts on the forks

Q5 (source of the ~106 ns cadence).

  • Q5-a, per-core serial write handshake: supported. (1) P6: the per-thread cadence is unchanged with 2/4/8 concurrent cores on one device (core-side aggregate 19/38/77 M/s), and with 4 cores the device PMU counts 38 M/s accepted with zero WQ-full drops (timing plus count). (2) P7: moving the core to the other socket adds 55-67 ns per write with the knee unchanged; the core's mesh position changes b from 127 to 188 cycles; a k-write MFENCE slope equals the cadence on an empty store buffer (manipulation plus a second timing signature). Refined form: cadence ≈ 27-31 ns + b(core) mesh cycles (+ ~15 core cycles), b = 127-188 for local cores, plus ≈ 35 ns + ~200-240 mesh cycles in total when crossing UPI. It is partly pipelined (~60 ns of overlap), not a full stop-and-wait round trip.
  • Q5-b, device entry rate: ruled out. One device accepted and completed 38 M writes/s from 4 cores with zero WQ-full and zero missing (PMU count plus completion records). The cadence depends on the submitting core's position, not on the device.
  • Q5-c, engine time: ruled out (again). Valid noop, noop without completion record and 64 B / 4 KiB memmove have the same cadence (the memmove streams that overflowed the WQ too, with the drop caveat).
