w2dev: device-side follow-ups to wave 1 (Q1 slots via ENQCMD, Q2 slot type, Q3 copy ceiling, Q4 batch engine)

Q1: per-engine slots seen by ENQCMD (shared WQ, E = 1..4)

Question. P9 found Retry onset = threshold + 6 with 2 engines. Is it threshold + 3E?

Predictions (written before running).

  • Q1-a (3 per engine, slot frees its WQ entry at dispatch): accepted before the first Retry = T + 3E = 35 / 38 / 41 / 44 at T = 32, and 76 at T = 64, E = 4. Occupancy right after the attempts = T exactly; PMU dispatch = 2 per engine (P10: 2 dispatched + 1 staged per engine).
  • Q1-b (a constant +6 in shared mode, e.g. a group-level staging buffer): onset = T + 6 for every E.
  • Q1-c (shared-mode WQ counts the descriptors in engines against the threshold): onset = T.

Method. w2_slots with --heads 0, fillers = 2 MiB memmove (as P9), single ENQCMD attempts, 5 reps per config. Configs w2-swq64-t32-e{1,2,3,4}.conf (one group, shared wq0.0 of size 64, threshold 32, E engines) and w2-swq64-t64-e{1,4}.conf. Validity: no completion before the counters are read (fill_done_after_reads = 0).

Runs (W2Q1/, 5 reps each, heaters 8, mesh 1.95-2.2 GHz at start and 2.2 at end, core 2.2-3.9 GHz): 20261101T024914Z-A-swq64-t32-e1-r1, …024915Z-A-swq64-t32-e1-r2, …024915Z-A-swq64-t32-e1-r3, …024916Z-A-swq64-t32-e2-r1, …024917Z-A-swq64-t32-e2-r2, …024917Z-A-swq64-t32-e2-r3, …024918Z-A-swq64-t32-e3-r1, …024919Z-A-swq64-t32-e3-r2, …024920Z-A-swq64-t32-e3-r3, …024920Z-A-swq64-t32-e4-r1, …024921Z-A-swq64-t32-e4-r2, …024922Z-A-swq64-t32-e4-r3, …024922Z-A-swq64-t64-e1-r1, …024923Z-A-swq64-t64-e1-r2, …024923Z-A-swq64-t64-e1-r3, …024924Z-A-swq64-t64-e4-r1, …024925Z-A-swq64-t64-e4-r2, …024925Z-A-swq64-t64-e4-r3, side B: …024931Z-B-swq64-t32-e1-sideB, …024933Z-B-swq64-t32-e4-sideB.   • The PMU counts 2 dispatched per engine, so the third per engine is accepted out of the WQ but not counted as dispatched (the same 2 + 1 split P10 found on a dedicated WQ).
  • Q1-b (constant +6) and Q1-c (onset = T) are ruled out.
  • P9's +6 was simply E = 2. The shared WQ behaves like the dedicated WQ (P4: C = size + 3E), with the threshold in place of the size.

Q2: is m = 3 per engine independent of descriptor type and size?

Predictions (written before running).

  • Q2-a (m = 3 descriptor slots per engine, in order, any type/size): with H slow heads (2 MiB memmove) already in the engines, fillers accepted before the first Retry = T + 3E − H, so heads + fillers = T + 3E for noop, 4 KiB, 64 KiB and 1 MiB fillers. No filler completes before the first head (fill_done_at_first_head = 0). Occupancy = T, dispatch 2 per engine.
  • Q2-b (the work pipeline lets short descriptors finish past a slow one): noop / 4 KiB fillers complete while heads still run (fill_done_at_first_head > 0), the WQ keeps draining, and the Retry onset moves later or disappears within the attempts.
  • Q2-c (m depends on the payload, e.g. a byte or read-buffer budget rather than a count): the capacity with small fillers differs from T + 3E without any filler completing, and per-engine dispatch differs from 2.

Method. w2_slots on w2-swq64-t32-e{1,4}.conf (T = 32): --heads H 2 MiB memmoves, then --settle-us 20, then 96 single ENQCMD attempts of fillers. H = E (one per engine), also 2E and 3E with noop fillers. The PMU snapshot after the heads gives the per-engine dispatch of the heads alone.

Runs (W2Q2/, 5 reps each; tag = E, H, filler): 20261101T024958Z-A-e1-h1-noopk, …024959Z-A-e1-h1-memmove4k, …024959Z-A-e1-h1-memmove64k, …025000Z-A-e1-h1-memmove1024k, …025001Z-A-e4-h4-noopk, …025001Z-A-e4-h4-memmove4k, …025002Z-A-e4-h4-memmove64k, …025002Z-A-e4-h4-memmove1024k, …025003Z-A-e4-h8-noopk, …025003Z-A-e4-h12-noopk, …025004Z-A-e1-h2-noopk, …025005Z-A-e1-h3-noopk.

Rep 0 of the E = 1 noop run accepted all 96 (the queue drained during the attempts and the head finished: the same first-rep anomaly as below) and is excluded; reps 1-4 give 67. So the capacity is size + 3E as well as threshold + 3E.
  • No filler had completed when the attempts ended, in any rep (fill_done_when_attempts_ended = 0), and no head had completed either. So the onset count is a clean capacity count.
  • The heads are dispatched round robin, one per engine (H = E: 1 per engine; H = 2E: 2 per engine).
  • After the attempts stop (occupancy read ~4 us and PMU ~11 us after the last attempt; the two reads are not simultaneous):
    • E = 1, one head: the WQ drains (noop: occupancy 0, PMU dispatch 34; 4 KiB: occupancy 5-25, dispatch 34; 64 KiB: occupancy 30, 4-5 dispatched; 1 MiB: occupancy 31-32, 1-2). This is confounded with the head finishing: the single 2 MiB head alone takes ~68 us (first head seen at 68-75 us after its submit), and settle (20 us) + PMU/occupancy reads after the heads
      • 27 us of attempts + the post-attempt reads end at about the same time. The head was seen done right after the reads in 4/5 (noop, 4 KiB), 2/5 (64 KiB) and 1/5 (1 MiB) reps. So the drain cannot be separated from the head's last microseconds or its completion; it is not evidence of short descriptors passing a running head.
    • E = 4, one head per engine (heads take ~260 us, still running): nothing moves within the reads for any filler type (occupancy 32, 1 head + 1 filler dispatched per engine).
    • fill_done_at_first_head (fillers seen done in the poll pass that first sees a head done) is 34 of 40 for noop fillers at E = 4 and 0 for 4 KiB-1 MiB. That pass reads the heads first and the fillers after, so noops written just after the heads are counted too: the completion order of noops vs heads is not resolved by this probe.
    • Two or three heads per engine: nothing moves, E = 1 and E = 4.
  • Rep 0 of each E = 1 run differs: the queue drained during the attempts, so 51 / 48 / 43 / 35 were accepted in total (first Retry still at 34 except 64 KiB: 37). [INFERENCE: first-touch of the ATC or the filler buffers; reps 1-4 are clean.]

Verdict.

  • Capacity: Q2-a survives. At the moment the device starts refusing, heads + fillers = T + 3E for noop, 4 KiB, 64 KiB and 1 MiB fillers and for 1, 2 or 3 slow heads per engine. Evidence: Retry count (manipulation of type, size and H) + occupancy = T + PMU dispatch. Whatever occupies the slots (a 2 MiB head or a noop), each engine takes exactly 3 before the WQ backs up. So the "3" is a count of descriptors, not bytes.
  • While the heads are still running (E = 4 rows, and every onset), nothing beyond 3 per engine is pulled out of the WQ for any filler type, so there is no sign of a byte budget (Q2-c not supported). Whether short descriptors can complete past a running head (Q2-b) is unresolved: the E = 1 drain coincides with the head finishing, and the E = 4 noop count is racy.
  • For the model: "3 per engine" holds for any descriptor type and size (noop to 2 MiB).

Q3: where is the ~30 GB/s copy ceiling?

Candidate limits and what each predicts (written before running).

candidate dsa0 + dsa2 at once (each local) both devices on node-0 memory read_buffers_allowed sweep src remote (higher read latency) dst remote transfer size
L1 read-buffer pool per device (Little: BW ≈ RB × 64 B / read latency) each keeps ~30 each keeps ~30 (unless memory saturates) BW ∝ RB up to the default 96 (no plateau, or plateau only near 96) drops by the latency ratio ~no change (posted writes) flat above a few KiB
L2 device link / IIO stack (fixed GB/s per device) each keeps ~30 each keeps ~30 flat, then a knee at small RB where L1 takes over no change while the link limits no change flat
L3 memory / socket fabric each keeps ~30 (separate sockets) the two share (sum ≈ 30) flat changes (UPI) changes flat
L4 one machine-wide stage the two share (sum ≈ 30) share flat – – –

Method. w2_bw: closed-loop memmove, window W outstanding per device, 200 ms per point, 3 reps; src and dst pools of 256 MiB each (> 60 MB LLC) bound with mbind (no MOF) and prefaulted; DSA PMU bytes (cl_processed, cl_write), pipefull, stall_nodesc, WQ-ready cycles per device; CHA clock per node during the window. Confs: cap-e4-wq128.conf for the default, w2-rb{N}-e{E}.conf (dedicated wq0.0 size 128, E engines, read_buffers_allowed N).

Verdict (per candidate):

  • L4 (one machine-wide stage) ruled out: two devices, each on its own memory, get 59.4 GB/s together, 2 × the single-device figure.
  • L1 in its pure form ruled out: bandwidth is not proportional to read buffers (concave: ×1.84 per doubling at small RB, ×1.13 for the last +50 %), and with the default 96 a remote source (longer read latency) does not lower it. Read buffers do limit bandwidth when restricted (13 GB/s at 16), and the limit is per group, not per engine (E = 1 and E = 4 coincide at 16, 32, 96). [INFERENCE: the concave curve is Little's law with a read latency that rises as the device approaches its ceiling; at 96 the pool is just about enough.]
  • L2 (a per-device ceiling) survives, at ~31.5 GB/s = one 32-byte unit per device clock. When src and dst are on different nodes, both devices reach 31.46 GB/s, and the PMU shows 0.98 × 32 B processed per device cycle (1.002 GHz × 32 B = 32.1 GB/s). Size (4 KiB-2 MiB), engine count (P4) and window do not move it. Two evidence types: manipulation (placement, size, E, W) + PMU ratio. [INFERENCE: a 32 B/clk datapath or device-fabric port at the ~1 GHz device clock; this run cannot tell which, because the device clock was not varied.]
  • L3 (memory on one node) is the measured candidate for the gap from 31.5 to ~29.8 and for the sharing in (b). With src and dst on the same node, bandwidth drops 5-8 %; split across nodes it reaches the device ceiling; and two devices whose traffic all lands on node 0 share ~27.7 GB/s (copy rate; ~55 GB/s of reads + writes at node 0). [INFERENCE: node-0 memory bandwidth (or its home agent) saturates near ~55-60 GB/s of read + write traffic; not confirmed by a memory-side counter.] The split is unfair: the local device gets 2/3.
  • So the "30 GB/s ceiling" seen in wave 1 is the device's ~31.5 GB/s limit, shaved by a few percent when read and write traffic hit the same memory node.

Q4: does one Batch run on a single engine?

Predictions (written before running).

  • Q4-a (one batch -> one engine): with K = 1 batch of W = 1024 × 4 KiB in flight, all 1024 works are counted by EV_NUM_DESC_FROM_BATCH on one engine per round; bandwidth ≈ 1/4 of the 4-engine figure (~7-9 GB/s) and the same on a 1-engine group. K = 4 spreads over the engines (~1024 each) and reaches ~30 GB/s on 4 engines but not on 1.
  • Q4-b (works of one batch spread across engines): K = 1 gives ~256 works per engine; the low bandwidth then comes from batch processing itself (descriptor-list fetch, serial issue).
  • Q4-c (one batch unit per device): K = 4 is no faster than K = 1.

Method. w2_batch_eng: K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} batches submitted back to back, wait for all, 20 rounds (+1 warm-up round dropped), PMU read around every round. Event sets: (i) from_batch@e0..3,disp_wq@e0..3, (ii) cl_processed@e0..3,pipefull@e0..3. Confs cap-e4-wq128.conf (4 engines) and w2-rb96-e1.conf (1 engine).

Runs (W2Q4/, 20 rounds each, mesh 1.9-2.2 GHz at start and 2.2 at end, all batch statuses 0x01 with 1024 descriptors completed, SWERROR unchanged): 20261101T025633Z-A-e4-w1024-4k-from_batch, …025635Z-A-e4-w1024-4k-cl_processed, …025635Z-A-e1-w1024-4k-from_batch, …025636Z-A-e1-w1024-4k-cl_processed, shared-source control (--shared-src, all works read the same 4 KiB as P8 did): …025707Z-A-e4-w1024-4k-sharedsrc, …025708Z-A-e1-w1024-4k-sharedsrc. Verdict.

  • "One batch runs on one engine": confirmed by direct count. All 1024 works of a batch are counted on a single engine; consecutive batches go round robin over the engines; K = 4 puts exactly one batch on each engine. Q4-b (spread) and Q4-c are ruled out.
  • But one engine is not what limits a batch. One batch on one engine reaches 30.7 GB/s, the same as 4 batches on 4 engines and the same on a 1-engine group. The engine running one batch even has its pipeline full only ~9 % of the time (12.3 k of ~136 k cycles). The ~31 GB/s device ceiling (Q3) is the limit at every K.
  • So the P8 observation ("W = 1024 reaches only ~1/4") is not explained by single-engine execution and does not reproduce: the unchanged p8_batch binary now gives 20.5 GB/s, and its W = 128 row also fell from 30.4 to 21.6. [INFERENCE: P8's payload layout (4096 separate heap Vecs of 4 KiB, so most destinations are not page- or line-aligned and span two pages) or its closed-loop structure costs bandwidth, and that cost varies between runs; not tested.]

Suggested model.js corrections (for the lead)

  • engine_slots: "3 per engine" now confirmed through ENQCMD as well: shared-WQ Retry onset = threshold + 3E for E = 1..4 (35/38/41/44 at T = 32, 67/76 at T = 64), occupancy = T, PMU 2 dispatched per engine. P9's "+6" = 3 × 2 engines. Add: the count is independent of what fills the slots (noop to 2 MiB) while the heads run. Whether a short descriptor can complete past a running long one in the same engine is still open (Q2 caveat).
  • Wave-1 forks Q3-a (C = size + const) and Q4-a (C = size + E·m) now hold for the shared WQ too, with the threshold in place of the size.
  • engines / bandwidth: replace "copy bandwidth limited in a shared stage" with: a per-device ceiling of ~31.5 GB/s ≈ 1 × 32 B per device clock (PMU cl_processed/cycle = 0.98 at the ceiling); each device keeps it when both run; independent of size (4 KiB-2 MiB), E and window. Same-node src+dst lowers it to ~29.7 (node memory, inferred); two devices on one node's memory share ~27.7 GB/s.
  • Read buffers: bandwidth vs read_buffers_allowed is concave (13 GB/s at 16, 20 at 32, 26 at 64, 30 at 96), per group, independent of E. 96 (the maximum) is needed for full bandwidth.
  • Batch: one Batch descriptor's works run on one engine (direct PMU count), batches are spread round robin, and one engine running one batch reaches the full device bandwidth. Retract the P8 "batch limited to 1/4 by one engine" inference.
  • Animation: the device data path moves ~32 B per ~1 ns device cycle.

Next questions

  1. Q2 ordering: can a short descriptor complete past a running long one in the same engine? Needs heads long enough to outlive every read (e.g. E = 1 with a longer head) and per-record completion timestamps without sysfs polling in between.
  2. Memory side of Q3: inside a lab run, read the IMC CAS counters during the local/local, split and node-0-shared runs to confirm ~55-60 GB/s of DRAM traffic, and record the memory population.
  3. Is the 32 B/clk ceiling the engine datapath or the device's fabric port? Two groups on one device with separate WQs cannot separate it (same device); varying the device clock would.
  4. Why does p8_batch give 20-22 GB/s for 4 KiB batches today (30 in P8)? Compare aligned vs heap-allocated destinations in the same binary.

W3 (follow-up round)

W3-1: memory side of Q3 (IMC CAS counters)

Events. UNC_M_CAS_COUNT.RD (EventCode 0x05, UMask 0xcf) and UNC_M_CAS_COUNT.WR (0x05, 0xf0), unit iMC, 64 B per CAS: intel/perfmon SPR/events/sapphirerapids_uncore.json V1.40 (fetched 2026-11-01). The kernel's uncore_imc_N/events/cas_count_read|write aliases encode the same values. Opened on every uncore_imc_N for both sockets on the PMU's cpumask CPU of that socket. w2_bw --imc reads them from a sampler thread (pinned to the last CPU of side A's node, off the workers) over the middle half of each 200 ms window, plus a 100 ms idle baseline with heaters on.

Predictions (before running).

  • (a) One device copying local at ~29.8 GB/s: node-0 DRAM read ≈ write ≈ 29.8 GB/s (≈ 60 GB/s total), node 1 ≈ idle. Split placement (31.5 GB/s): ~31.5 read on one node, ~31.5 write on the other.
  • (b) Both devices on node-0 memory: node-0 read ≈ write ≈ 27.7 GB/s (sum ≈ 55 GB/s). If this is the node's practical ceiling, it should be about equal to or below the read + write total of case (a); if (b) is far below (a), the shared limit is not DRAM (e.g. home agent / UPI).
  • Alternative: DRAM traffic exceeds copy bytes (e.g. write-allocate reads, directory updates), read > copy rate.

Runs (W3IMC/, 1 MiB, W = 16, 200 ms windows, 2 reps, heaters 8 on both nodes, mesh 2.2 GHz): 20261101T034035Z-AB-both-local, …034039Z-AB-both-node0mem, …034042Z-AB-split-A. Idle baseline (heaters on, no copy): 0.02-0.04 GB/s read and write per node.

W3-2: why P8's batch bandwidth does not reproduce

Facts going in. P8 (P8/20261101T022317Z-A-mm4096-rcfalse-v3, core 3.8 GHz): 4 KiB batch works, closed loop, W = 128 window 32 → 30.4 GB/s, W = 1024 window 4 → 9.4. Same binary today (W2Q4/20261101T025721Z-A-p8-repro-w128-1024, core 2.2 GHz): 21.6 and 20.5. w2_batch_eng (page-aligned mbind'd buffers, rounds): 30.7-31.0 at every K. P8's payload: one heap Vec of 4 KiB as the source for every work and 4096 separate heap Vecs of 4 KiB as destinations (glibc chunks of 4096 + 16 B: destinations are 16-B aligned, cycle through 4 offsets mod 64, and almost all straddle two pages). THP is madvise (heap not madvised, so 4 KiB pages either way).

Predictions (before running).

  • H-a (layout): P8's heap layout reproduced inside w2_batch_eng drops below ~31 GB/s, and the translation counters (EV_ATS_RSP_*, ATC alloc/no-alloc) per work rise (an extra page per straddling destination), or the line misalignment (partial first/last lines) costs bandwidth. Synthetic layouts separate the two: offset 64 stride 4096 (page-straddling, line-aligned) vs offset 16 (both) vs offset 0 (neither).
  • H-b (loop structure / core clock): P8's closed loop with aligned buffers drops; the layout does not matter.
  • H-c (NUMA placement of P8's heap pages): heap pages on the far node (checked with move_pages).
  • Run-to-run variation of P8 (9.4 vs 20.5, 30.4 vs 21.6) follows whichever factor is not fixed by the binary (heap phase, page placement, core clock).

W3-3: can a short descriptor finish ahead of a long one on the same engine?

Predictions (before running).

  • O-a (in-order per engine): on a 1-engine group, no noop / 4 KiB filler is seen before the 2 MiB head completes (fillers_before_first_head = 0); fillers finish within ~1 us after the head.
  • O-b (two independent pipeline slots): with one head, fillers flow through the free slot and finish within a few us of their submit, long before the head (~68 us); with two heads (both slots busy) they wait for a head.
  • O-c (a batch head is processed differently): a Batch head (512 × 4 KiB) behaves like O-a or O-b independently of the memmove result.

Method. w3_order on w2-rb96-e1.conf (dedicated WQ 128, 1 engine): heads, 5 us gap, then 16 fillers, all MOVDIR64B; poll every record in passes (heads first), no sysfs/PMU reads while polling; 10 reps (+1 warm-up rep dropped).

Runs (W3ORD/, 10 reps each, mesh 1.9-2.2 → 2.2 GHz, core 2.2 GHz, all statuses 0x01): 20261101T034442Z-A-h1-noop, …034444Z-A-h1-4k, …034444Z-A-h2-noop, …034445Z-A-h3-noop, …034446Z-A-h1batch-noop, …034446Z-A-h1batch-4k; witness reruns …034516Z-A-w-h1-noop, …034516Z-A-w-h1-4k, …034519Z-A-w-h1batch-noop. Pass length ~50 ns. Witness: when a filler record is first seen, every head is re-read at once; the filler counts as "passed" only if all heads still read 0. (The plain pass comparison reads heads before fillers, so a head that lands between the two loops is credited to the next pass; it produced the sporadic "passed" counts in the first h1-noop run and is not used.)

  • Behind a work descriptor, no. On one engine, noops and 4 KiB copies submitted 5 us after a 2 MiB memmove complete only when it completes (0 of 160 pass, witness-checked). [INFERENCE: the 67 us spacing at the full ~30 GB/s fits one copy streaming at a time, but completion records cannot exclude overlapping execution inside the pipeline.] This matches P12's "waves of E" and Q2's "nothing passes while heads run".
  • Behind a Batch, yes. Fillers submitted after a 512-work batch complete at 12-15 us, ~53 us before the batch (160 / 160, witness-checked; PMU: 512 from_batch + 16 disp_wq). The engine interleaves WQ descriptors with the works of a batch it is processing (O-c).
  • For the model: per-engine order holds for WQ descriptors; a batch does not block the engine's queue.
