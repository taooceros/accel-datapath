Fault trap found on the way (affects every memmove probe)

  • Symptom. With every page prefaulted, memmove works still completed with status 0x03 (page fault, BOF=0). It started about 1 s into the process, and from then on every batch failed.
    • Run with prefault, without the fix: P8/20261101T021734Z-A-mm4096-rctrue-prefault. The first failed work's work_status_hist shows 0x03.
  • Cause. Automatic NUMA balancing is on (/proc/sys/kernel/numa_balancing = 1). After its scan delay (~1 s) it marks pages PROT_NONE. Pages that only the device touches are never re-faulted by the CPU, so DSA faults on them.
  • Manipulation. Same binary and arguments, with a task mempolicy that has no MPOL_F_MOF (pin_mempolicy, so task_numa_work skips the VMAs): 0 errors in every row.
    • Run with the fix: P8/20261101T021916Z-A-mm4096-rctrue-mempol.
  • Reported to Main. It is separate from the touched(len, 0) calloc problem; both need fixing.
  • Runs before 021916Z that show errors are not used below.

P8: Batch descriptor (one 64-byte write carries W work descriptors)

Question. Is the ~106 ns cadence a property of each 64-byte write (the submit path), or of the engines' work rate?

Predictions (written before running; model.js P8 and the assignment):

  • The cadence is in the 64-byte write path (Q5-a or Q5-b). Then each Batch descriptor costs the same ~106 ns, and per-work throughput rises about W × 9.4 M/s, far above the single-descriptor rate, until something else saturates.
  • The limit is the engines. Then per-work throughput barely changes with W.

Method (p8_batch, config 4-engine.conf: dedicated WQ size 128, 4 engines, max_batch_size 1024):

  • Submit cost per top-level descriptor. 126 descriptors (Batch, or single for W=1) are sent back to back, which stays below the WQ depth, so nothing is dropped. Fill region: submits 0..118, below the store-buffer knee. Steady region: submits 118..126, above it. 10 reps.
  • Throughput. A closed loop keeps window descriptors outstanding and reuses a slot once its completion record arrives. It runs ≥ 262144 works or ≥ 4000 tops, 3 reps.
    • Reported: work M/s, top-level M/s, and the fraction of time the CPU waited for a free slot.
  • memmove destinations. In the closed (throughput) loop every in-flight work has its own destination buffer (pool of 4096 buffers, indexed by slot × W + j). The window is capped at 4096/W, so two works in flight never share a destination: window 32 at W=128 and 4 at W=1024. The 126-descriptor stream (submit-cost measurement) does not have this guarantee: for 126 × W > 4096 (W ≥ 64) its works reuse destinations. That affects only device-side work, not the posted submit timing reported from it.
  • W = 1 is single descriptors, not a Batch. GENCAP is 0x40915f0107, so Batch1 Support (bit 52) is 0. A Batch of 1 completes with status 0x14 ("descriptor count out of range"); a Batch of 2 completes with 0x01.
    • Shown by P8/20261101T021521Z-A-noop-rc (batch_small). The final runs do not submit it.
  • Validity of the final matrix. Every top-level completion record (Batch or single) has status 0x01. A successful Batch status means every work in it succeeded. Per-work records exist only in the work-RC runs, and there they are all 0x01 too. There are 0 missing descriptors and SWERROR does not change.

Survived: the cadence is in the 64-byte write path (timing signature + direct manipulation).

  • Submit cost. A Batch descriptor costs the same as a single descriptor at the same mesh clock. Above the knee it is 105.5-107.6 ns for every W from 1 to 1024 and every op.
    • The device only accepts the write; the cost does not depend on how much work the write carries (2 to 1024 works, up to 4 MiB of data).
    • Below the knee a submit costs 4.0-8.1 ns (~4 ns, rising to 5-8 ns with work RCs at W ≥ 64).
  • Throughput. Up to W=16 (noop, no RC), the device takes one top-level descriptor per cadence (9.41 M tops/s). Work throughput is exactly W × 9.41: 150.6 M/s at W=16, 16× the single rate. It saturates at ~200 M works/s for W ≥ 32.
    • So the 4 engines can do ≥ 20× more noops than the submit path delivers. The engine is not what limits single descriptors (Q5-c, already ruled out, now ruled out with a 20× margin).
  • The "engine limit" prediction holds only where real data movement dominates.
    • 4 KiB memmove is bandwidth-bound at ~30 GB/s: 7.4 M/s for every W, the same as single descriptors (7.2 M/s). Batching cannot help when the engine or memory is the limit.
    • 64 B memmove saturates at 21.5 M/s (no RC) or 25.6 M/s (RC), ~2.3-2.8× the single rate.

Secondary observations.

  • Per-work completion records cost throughput. With per-work RCs, noop throughput caps at ~90 M/s, versus 200 M/s without.
    • There is a reproducible dip at W=8-16: 35-43 M/s, against 37.5 M/s at W=4. It repeats in P8/20261101T021601Z-A-noop-sweep-rctrue (W=6 39.5, W=8 36.0, W=16 38.0 M/s).
    • [INFERENCE] The 32-byte record writes, two per cache line and written by different engines, become the bottleneck.
  • One large batch seems to run in one engine. At window=4, W=1024 4 KiB memmove reaches only 9.2-9.4 GB/s, about a quarter of the 30 GB/s that 4 engines give.
    • [INFERENCE] A batch's works run in one engine with limited parallelism. Parallelism comes from multiple batches in flight.
    • The W=1024 memmove rows are window-limited (only 4 in flight, because destinations must stay distinct). Do not compare them with the other rows.

Caveats.

  • W=32 noop, no RC, has a steady-region p10 of 4 ns: in one rep the 8-submit steady region did not reach the knee. The median is unaffected.
  • The closed-loop CPU wait fraction for singles is 0.4-0.7, yet throughput equals the cadence. The stall moved from the submit instruction into the completion poll: completion i can only be written after descriptor i leaves the store buffer.
  • Discarded runs:
    • 021521-021738: calloc and NUMA-balancing faults.
    • 021900: stale binary.
    • 022204-022252 (-final, -final2): destinations shared across in-flight batches, or stream overflow drops.

Next question. P8 cannot separate Q5-a (per-core write round trip) from Q5-b (device-entry rate), because both predict this result. P9 and P7 (two cores) can.

P9: shared WQ + ENQCMD

Question. ENQCMD is non-posted, so its time is one round trip to the device. Is that round trip about the MOVDIR64B cadence (Q5-b, device entry)? Where does Retry start compared with the WQ size (direct device capacity)? Does ENQCMD show the ~116 knee (Q1)?

Predictions (model.js P9 and the assignment, written before running):

  • Q1-b (store buffer): ENQCMD shows no knee at ~114-116, and Retry starts at the WQ size.
  • Q5-b (device-entry cadence): the ENQCMD round trip ≈ the MOVDIR64B cadence (~106 ns at 2.2 GHz mesh).

Method (p9_enqcmd; dedicated wq0.0 of size 64 on 2 engines, plus shared wq0.1 on 2 engines):

  • ENQCMD isolated (lat). One ENQCMD timed with rdtscp, then wait for its completion, then 1 µs gap, so the queue is empty every time. 2000 samples heated, 200 per idle cycle.
  • References in the same process.
    • ENQCMD back to back: steady region of 400 submits, starting at 150.
    • MOVDIR64B isolated, and MOVDIR64B followed by MFENCE.
    • MOVDIR64B steady cadence on the dedicated WQ.
  • Idle mesh. The probe's own polling raises the mesh within ~1 s.
    • With --heaters 0 and a long loop the mesh stays at 2.2 GHz: P9/20261101T022408Z-AB-lat-mesh-sweep, which is therefore NOT an idle-mesh measurement.
    • Fix: sleep 2.5 s, then take a short sample (F1 method), with --side both.
    • Mesh GHz is measured by the CHA clock over each ≥ 0.5 ms isolated loop. The CHA reading over ~40 µs bursts is not trustworthy: it reported 3-5 GHz.
  • Retry onset (onset). Single ENQCMD attempts (no retry loop) of 2 MiB memmoves. These are slow enough that nothing completes during the ~80 µs of attempts (completed_when_attempts_ended = 0). The index of the first Retry is the number of descriptors the device holds when it starts refusing. 5 reps per config.
  • Knee (knee). X1-style: prefill K, time submit K+1. ENQCMD on the shared WQ and MOVDIR64B on the dedicated WQ, same K list, 30 iterations each.

Fit (two-clock decomposition):

  • MOVDIR64B steady cadence, from the matched-core rows (core ≈ 2.1-2.2): (242.3 − 110.5) / (1/0.80 − 1/2.20) → ~166 mesh cycles + 35 ns. This is F1's 169 + 36. Core sensitivity is small: 110.5 → 121.9 ns from a 2.2 to a ~0.8 GHz core.
  • ENQCMD isolated: ~163 mesh cycles + ~130 core cycles + ~98 ns.
    • Checks: 3.9 / 2.2 GHz core at 2.2 GHz mesh → 205 / 231 ns. Cold core at 2.2 GHz mesh → 336 (model 337). Idle mesh, core 2.14 → 361 (model 362).
    • About 36 of the 130 core cycles are rdtscp overhead (MOVDIR64B isolated: 10 / 17 / 46 ns at 3.9 / 2.2 / 0.79 GHz).
  • ENQCMD back to back ≈ isolated minus the rdtscp overhead: 186 vs 205 ns, 341 vs 361 ns. ENQCMDs from one core do not overlap; one is outstanding at a time (timing signature).

Verdict for Q5-b ("ENQCMD round trip ≈ cadence"): its literal prediction fails. The round trip is 1.9-2.2× the cadence with a hot mesh (205-231 vs 106-110 ns) and 1.5× with an idle mesh (361 vs 242).

  • Signature: the mesh-clocked part is the same in both: ~163 mesh cycles for the ENQCMD round trip and ~166 for the posted cadence.
  • [INFERENCE] The posted cadence pays the full mesh-side round trip of one 64-byte write per submit. It does not pay the ~98 ns that scales with neither clock (PCIe/IIO-side or the device's accept decision), nor the core-side ENQCMD cost.
  • This fits Q5-a (each core's posted MMIO write waits for one mesh-level credit/ack round trip before the next leaves) better than Q5-b (a device acceptance rate). Under Q5-b the device's own response time would be in the cadence.
  • Not excluded: an IIO-side acceptance stage clocked by the mesh, shared by cores. P7 (two cores, same device) separates the two.
  • MOVDIR64B + MFENCE isolated (~187 mesh cycles + ~76 ns) is the time until one posted write is acknowledged. It is about one cadence plus ~35-40 ns, which fits the same picture.

Verdict. Retry starts at threshold + 6 in all four configs. The WQ size matters only because the threshold is capped at it.

  • With threshold = size (the user-space usable capacity), the device refuses at size + 6: 38 and 70. Both are far below the ~114-116 knee (manipulation of size and of threshold, with counts).
  • [INFERENCE] The +6 is constant across sizes 32/64 and thresholds 1-64. These are descriptors already pulled out of the WQ into the 2 engines: ~3 per engine, which frees their WQ entries (Q3-a).
    • This matches the ~11 extra seen in X4 with 4 engines (139 − 128 ≈ 3 × 4 − 1) and supports Q4-a (per-engine slots, m ≈ 3).
    • A 4-engine shared-WQ run would test it (prediction: threshold + 12).
  • The prediction "Retry starts at the WQ size" survives, refined to WQ threshold + engine slots. The device's capacity is not the ~114 that stalls the core.

  • The same holds in the other three configs (swq32, thr16, thr1): ENQCMD is flat at 187-233 ns for every K, while MOVDIR64B steps up at K=112-116.
  • ENQCMD prefill and timed submits never retried, and nothing was missing or failed: the noops drain faster than one ENQCMD round trip.
  • The ENQCMD trace (every submit timed, K up to 160) is flat at 182-233 ns after the first submit. The first submit costs 380-730 ns.

Verdict.

  • ENQCMD shows no knee. Survives, but it is weak evidence for Q1-b by itself: ENQCMD holds one outstanding write per core, so it cannot fill any buffer, store buffer or downstream.
  • The stronger Q1 evidence is two observations together:
    • The knee location is 112-116 with a 64-entry dedicated WQ. X1 reference runs on this config and on the 128-entry 4-engine.conf both put it at ~116: P9/20261101T022747Z-B-x1-ref-p9-swq64 and P9/20261101T022748Z-B-x1-ref-4-engine.
    • The device starts refusing at 38/70, not 114.
    • Together they say the ~114 buffer is not WQ or device capacity. This fits Q1-b (core store buffer) and is against a device-entry buffer sized by the WQ (Q1-c).

Caveat (open). The MOVDIR64B plateau above the knee is ~390-440 ns in p9_enqcmd's knee loop, but ~94-99 ns in x1_knee on the same config and side.

  • It does not come from using ENQCMD. It is still ~389 ns in a process that never executes ENQCMD: P9/20261101T022907Z-B-knee-mov-only-noenq. In P9/20261101T022908Z-A-knee-mov-then-enq it is ~424 ns.
  • So something in this probe's loop makes the store buffer appear to drain in bursts of ~4. It is not understood.
  • The knee location agrees with X1 and the ENQCMD rows are unaffected. Do not use P9's MOVDIR64B plateau numbers.

Discarded P9 runs.

  • 022408Z-AB-lat-mesh-sweep: the mesh was not idle at h=0 (2.20 GHz measured).
  • 022509Z-AB-lat-idle-vs-heated: stale binary; the build had failed.
