# Submit occupancy payload sweep with hardware-observed counts, 2026-06-02

## Question

Extend the Experiment 1 payload-size sweep to record how many submitted requests are actually visible through completion polling, and include 4 KiB and 64 KiB payloads.

## Method

Built release `hw-eval` and ran `submit-occupancy` on `/dev/dsa/wq0.0`, pinned to core 0.

Workloads:

- `noop`
- shared-payload memmove: 4 KiB, 64 KiB, 1 MiB, 10 MiB, 100 MiB, 1 GiB

Sweep:

- K: `96,114,128,160,192,224,256,288,320`
- `gap_tsc`: `0,1,10,25,50,100,150,200,300`
- requested iterations: `10`

Raw JSON now records `hardware_observed` in each `trace_outcomes` point. It is the count of submitted completion records that were visible through polling/scanning after the row's drain attempt.

Artifacts:

- Raw JSON: `hw-eval/results/2026-06-02-payload-size-sweep-observed-iter10-perk/`
- Summary CSV: `docs/report/benchmarking/049.submit_occupancy_payload_observed_sweep_iter10.summary.csv`

## Drain status

- `noop`, 4 KiB, 64 KiB, 1 MiB, and 10 MiB: all rows collected 10 samples and the drain sentinel completed.
- 100 MiB and 1 GiB: each row stopped after one sample with `drain_sentinel_completed=false`. This avoids reusing buffers that may still be visible to hardware after a dedicated-WQ burst.

## Interpretation

The submit-latency knee and the hardware-observed count answer different questions.

- The extra-submit knee still appears near K=114 without pacing for all normal rows, so the first latency increase is still a submit-admission / descriptor-consumption effect.
- Hardware-observed counts reveal a separate payload/completion capacity signal. At K=320, visible completions saturate around 150 for 64 KiB, 131-132 for 1 MiB/10 MiB, about 17 for 100 MiB, and 1 for 1 GiB within the bounded drain observation window.
- Larger `gap_tsc` can move the submit-latency knee right, but it does not make huge payload completions appear quickly. The 100 MiB and 1 GiB rows remain completion-limited / undrainable under this dedicated-WQ burst pattern.

So the updated data separates the two mechanisms: submit admission explains the first extra-submit latency knee; payload size governs how many completions are actually observed after the burst.
